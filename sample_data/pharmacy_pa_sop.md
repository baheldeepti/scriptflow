# Sunset Community Pharmacy — Prior Authorization SOP

*Standard Operating Procedure for Prior Authorization Workflow*
*Version 2.1 — Effective Date: January 2026*
*Document Owner: Pharmacy Operations*

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) defines the workflow, responsibilities, and timelines for handling prescription claim rejections that require prior authorization (PA) at Sunset Community Pharmacy. It applies to all licensed pharmacists, pharmacy technicians, and pharmacy interns involved in dispensing operations.

## 2. Patient Safety Priority Tiers

Prior authorization cases are triaged into three priority tiers based on clinical urgency. All staff must classify cases at intake.

### Tier 1 — CRITICAL (4-hour SLA, expedited PA)
Medications where delay carries imminent harm risk:
- Insulin and rapid-acting glucose-lowering agents for known T1DM
- Anticoagulants (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, edoxaban)
- Antiepileptics in known seizure disorder
- Active oncology regimens
- HIV antiretrovirals (continuity)
- Post-transplant immunosuppressants
- Antibiotics for active bacterial infection
- Cardiac medications post-MI/HF

### Tier 2 — HIGH (24-hour SLA, expedited if indicated)
Chronic disease medications where >72-hour delay risks deterioration:
- Diabetes medications (oral and injectable) including GLP-1 RAs
- Antihypertensives in patients with established CV disease
- Asthma/COPD controllers
- Mental health medications (continuity)
- Specialty biologics

### Tier 3 — STANDARD (72-hour SLA)
- Lifestyle medications
- Cosmetic
- Non-urgent refills
- New starts without immediate clinical urgency

## 3. Workflow Steps

### 3.1 Intake (within 30 minutes of rejection)
1. Pharmacy technician reviews the rejection notice on the pharmacy management system.
2. Tag with priority tier (above).
3. Open a PA case in the pharmacy workflow tool.
4. If Tier 1 → notify pharmacist on duty immediately and page the prescriber.

### 3.2 Classification and Documentation Gathering
The pharmacy technician (or the AI assistant) must:
1. Identify the rejection code and subcode (e.g. "75-A — Step Therapy").
2. Identify the payer's documentation requirements.
3. Pull from the patient's medication history (pharmacy system, e-prescribe history, care plan):
   - Prior medication trials (drug, dates, outcome)
   - Active diagnoses (ICD-10)
   - Recent labs relevant to the medication class
   - Any known contraindications to alternatives
4. Identify gaps that require contact with the prescriber.

### 3.3 Provider Outreach
- Contact prescriber's office with a structured request: rejection reason, what is missing, what is needed.
- Use the standard PA request fax cover sheet. Do not transmit PHI by unsecured email.

### 3.4 PA Form Submission
- Use the payer's preferred submission channel (ePA portal preferred over fax).
- Attach all supporting clinical documentation.
- Document submission timestamp, confirmation number, and assigned reviewer.

### 3.5 Follow-up
- Call payer at SLA midpoint (e.g. 12 hours for Tier 2) to confirm receipt.
- On approval: notify patient, dispense, document.
- On denial: review denial reason; consider peer-to-peer or formal appeal if clinically warranted.

## 4. Patient Communication Standards

### 4.1 Initial Notification
When a PA is required, the patient must be notified within 4 hours by phone or secure text. Use plain language:
- What happened ("Your insurance needs to approve this medication first.")
- What we are doing ("We are working with your prescriber to submit the paperwork.")
- Expected timeline ("This usually takes 24 to 72 hours.")
- What they should do ("Do not start any new medication unless your provider tells you to. Call us if your symptoms get worse.")

### 4.2 Bridge Therapy and Substitution
If the patient is at risk of harm from delay:
- Tier 1 medications: contact prescriber for emergency bridge supply per state rules.
- Tier 2 chronic: discuss with prescriber whether a covered alternative is clinically acceptable.
- Document the conversation.

## 5. Escalation Triggers

Escalate to the supervising pharmacist when:
- The patient has any Tier 1 indication.
- The PA has been pending more than the SLA.
- The denial reason appears clinically inappropriate.
- The patient reports clinical deterioration during the wait.
- The patient is at risk of treatment abandonment due to cost or fatigue.

## 6. Documentation Requirements

Every PA case file must contain:
1. Rejection notice (original)
2. Classified rejection category and priority tier
3. List of documentation gathered
4. Provider communication log (date, time, person spoken to)
5. PA form submission proof
6. Outcome (approval/denial)
7. Patient communication log
8. If denied: appeal disposition
9. Total time from rejection to dispensed (or abandoned/transferred)

## 7. Performance Metrics (Monthly)

Pharmacy operations tracks:
- % of PAs resolved within SLA by tier
- % of patients abandoning prescription after PA
- Median time from rejection to dispense
- Appeal success rate
- Patient-reported satisfaction

## 8. Use of AI-Assisted Tools

Where AI assistants (such as ScriptFlow) are used to draft PA documents and recommended actions:
- All AI-generated forms and clinical justifications must be reviewed by a licensed pharmacist before submission.
- AI-generated content does not replace human clinical judgment.
- All AI tool interactions must be logged for auditability.
- AI tools must use only synthetic or de-identified data during testing and demonstrations.
